# Lit sweep 1/5 — Multi-domain GFM pretraining & data-mixture/scaling laws

*Deep literature agent report, 2026-07-25. Post-Jan-2026 arXiv swept explicitly. Verbatim.*

## Closest papers (ranked by threat/relevance)

1. **LLM as GNN: Graph Vocabulary Learning for TAG Foundation Models (PromptGFM)** — arXiv Mar 2025 — 2503.03313 — LLM-as-GNN vocabulary; Sec. 5.2 empirically observes "incorporating a single external graph enhances performance, adding multiple graphs can lead to negative transfer," with co-training deteriorating as more cross-domain data is added ("domain-specific knowledge conflicts"). RELATION: adjacent but leaves our claim open — the single closest empirical observation to non-additive composition, but framed as a limitation in one table; no per-source attribution, no ladder/staircase, no functional characterization.
2. **SAMGPT** — WWW 2025 — 2502.05424 — Text-free multi-domain pretraining with structure tokens; Table 4 "data ablation" scales source domains 1→4 on Cora (40.4→46.0), claiming monotone gains, and notes GraphPrompt/GCOPE "often perform worse as more datasets are added." RELATION: contradicts our claim (composition is monotone-additive for them) — but only 4 tiny graphs, no target-in-corpus control, no per-source attribution; the GCOPE/GraphPrompt aside actually supports us.
3. **Better with Less: Data-Active Graph Pre-training (APT)** — NeurIPS 2023 — arXiv 2311.01038 — Identifies a "curse of big data" in graph pretraining (more graphs ≠ better downstream) and selects few instructive graphs. RELATION: adjacent but leaves our claim open — establishes non-monotonicity and motivates selection, but offers a selector method, not a max/best-donor law, no staircase or donor matrix.
4. **Do Neural Scaling Laws Exist on Graph SSL?** — arXiv Aug 2024 — 2408.11243 — Negative result: downstream performance "merely fluctuates" across data/model scales for graph SSL. RELATION: methodological anchor — supports "scale isn't the axis"; our max-composition gives a mechanism (coverage, not volume) that could explain flat/pseudo-scaling curves.
5. **GCOPE (All in One and One for All)** — KDD 2024 — arXiv 2402.09834 — Coordinators unify multi-graph corpora; motivated by single-source negative transfer (Fig. 1) and "brutal combination of source datasets still failed to enhance performance"; reports 3–38% gains. RELATION: adjacent + citable contrast — claims method turns mixing into positive transfer, but never varies source count, no per-source analysis.
6. **One Model for One Graph** — arXiv Dec 2024 (rev. May 2025) — 2412.00315 — Abandons the single cross-domain model for a bank of per-graph expert models with gating, because one GNN "fail[s] to capture diverse graph patterns across domains." RELATION: adjacent but leaves our claim open — same diagnosis (specialists win), opposite response (architecture, not analysis); no composition characterization.
7. **MDGMIX** — arXiv **May 2026** — 2605.25771 — Finds "significant data redundancy in multi-domain graph pre-training"; mixes boundary subgraphs for efficiency. RELATION: adjacent — redundancy echoes our "8th graph buys nothing," but framed as compute waste; no donor structure, ladder, or order analysis.
8. **AnyGraph** — arXiv 2024 (ICLR'25 sub) — 2408.10700 — MoE GFM over 38 datasets claiming "scaling law emergence" (more data/params → better + emergent generalization). RELATION: contradicts our claim — the strongest "more data helps" aggregate claim; never separates in-corpus coverage from genuine cross-domain gain.
9. **GraphFM (multi-graph pretraining)** — TMLR 2025 — arXiv 2407.11907 — Perceiver encoder over 152 graphs; "first scaling laws for multi-graph pretraining"; claims diverse-corpus > single-source. RELATION: citable contrast — scaling curves are in tokens/model size, not number of domains; no attribution or negative-transfer analysis.
10. **Position: GFMs are Already Here** — ICML 2024 — arXiv 2402.02216 — "Similarity between pre-training data and downstream task data serves as a prerequisite for the data scaling on graphs"; warns scaling fails when data violates transferable principles (arxiv vs arxiv-year example). RELATION: methodological anchor — licenses our divergence-predicts-transfer framing; we supply the quantitative law it lacks.
11. **Data mixture laws line: Data Mixing Laws** (2403.16952, ICLR 2025), **DoReMi** (2305.10429), **RegMix** (2407.01492, ICLR 2025), **BiMix** (2405.14908), **Scaling Laws for Optimal Data Mixtures** (2507.09404, NeurIPS 2025). RELATION: methodological anchors — none report max/best-donor composition; our discrete "MAX over components" is a qualitatively different functional form.
12. **Curse of multilinguality line: XLM-R** (1911.02116), **negative interference** (2010.03017 — monolingual beats multilingual), **Chang et al., 250 languages** (2311.09205 — adding languages eventually degrades all). RELATION: methodological anchor — the best-established "mixture ≤ best component + dilution tax" precedent, in language not graphs.
13. **ULTRA** — ICLR 2024 — arXiv 2310.04562 — KG foundation model: mixture of diverse small graphs beats one large graph (relational diversity drives inductive generalization). RELATION: citable contrast — diversity-helps claim in KGs; we show for interaction graphs diversity only helps via coverage.
14. **Subgraph Pooling** (2402.08907 — negative transfer on graphs even between similar source/target, structure-driven), **W2PGNN** (2303.16458 — when to pretrain, predicts feasibility from generative similarity), **MentorGNN** (2208.09905 — learned curriculum over pretraining graphs), **GraphKeeper** (2511.00097, NeurIPS 2025 — graph domain-incremental learning, forgetting across sequential domains). ## Answers to the four questions

**Q1 — What do GFM papers claim about adding domains?** Almost all report aggregate "more/diverse data helps": OFA (2310.00149) trains jointly cross-domain with no source-count analysis; OpenGraph (2403.01121) and AnyGraph (2408.10700) claim generalization/scaling-law emergence; UniGraph (2402.13630), UniGraph2 (2502.00806), GFT (2411.06070 — "reduces the risk of negative transfer" via tree vocabulary), GraphCLIP (2410.10329) likewise. PRODIGY (2305.12600) itself pretrains on one corpus (MAG240M or Wiki) — its ablations are objective-level, never data-mixture-level. (a) Source-count scaling curves: only SAMGPT (2502.05424, 1→4 domains, claims monotone) and MDGCL (2506.22510, Jun 2025, claims improvement with more domains) — both tiny-scale, neither controls for target-graph coverage. (b) Per-source attribution / donor-receiver matrices / leave-one-out: **none found**. GCOPE explicitly aggregates over 9 sources with no per-source breakdown. (c) Negative transfer/dilution: acknowledged as motivation (GCOPE, GFT, MDGPT 2405.13934, Hypergraph FM 2503.01203) and observed empirically in PromptGFM 2503.03313 (single graph helps, multiple hurt) and in SAMGPT's report that GCOPE/GraphPrompt degrade with more datasets — but always as a problem their method fixes, never characterized.

**Q2 — Graph data scaling laws.** 2402.02054 fits model/data scaling on single-task graph data (collapse via overfitting); 2408.11243 finds **no** scaling laws for graph SSL; GraphFM 2407.11907 fits multi-graph scaling laws (tokens, not domains); UniAug 2406.01899 shows cross-domain data scaling for a structure-only diffusion augmentor, with the caveat that pretraining–downstream similarity is a prerequisite (echoed by position paper 2402.02216); APT 2311.01038 shows the curse of big data. **No one has characterized multi-source composition as max/best-donor or fit any per-target functional form over corpus composition.**

**Q3 — LLM mixture laws as anchors.** Data Mixing Laws (2403.16952) fit smooth exponential-family functions of mixture proportions; BiMix (2405.14908) bivariate proportion×volume; RegMix (2407.01492) regression over mixtures; DoReMi (2305.10429) minimax reweighting; 2507.09404 additive law where mixture only shifts the loss floor. All assume smooth, interpolating composition — none report max-like/best-component behavior. The genuine "mixture ≈ best component (minus tax)" precedent is multilingual: negative interference (2010.03017: monolingual > multilingual) and the curse of multilinguality (1911.02116; 2311.09205: past a point, adding languages hurts everyone). Multi-task analogs: negative-transfer surveys (1811.09751).

**Q4 — Scoop check.** No 2025/2026 paper documents best-donor/max composition, per-target entry-jump staircases, or order-robustness of a data ladder for graph pretraining. Closest threats, all partial: PromptGFM 2503.03313 (one-table observation of non-monotonicity, Mar 2025); MDGMIX 2605.25771 (**May 2026**, redundancy framing); One-Model-One-Graph 2412.00315 (specialist experts as architecture); SAMGPT (opposite claim at toy scale); GFM benchmark 2603.10033 (**Mar 2026** — abstract shows cross-domain scenario grids, not composition laws; double-check its repo). Order-robustness of multi-source pretraining appears only in continual-learning form (task-order matters: 2205.13323; GraphKeeper 2511.00097), which our interleaved-vs-sequential + order-invariance results complement, not duplicate.

## Verdict

**Occupied territory:** the bare existence of cross-domain negative transfer on graphs (2402.08907, GCOPE, PromptGFM), "more data ≠ better" (2311.01038, 2408.11243), and specialist-beats-generalist as a design intuition (2412.00315, GP2F 2602.11629 Feb 2026). We cannot present any of these as new.

**Genuinely open (our contribution):** (i) the **functional characterization** of composition as per-target MAX over components with a quantified dilution tax; (ii) the **interpolation-ladder staircase** with entry-jump statistics and post-entry level order-invariance (21/21, p≈5e-7) — no graph paper varies corpus composition this systematically; (iii) the **donor/receiver asymmetry matrix** and its prediction by feature-distribution divergence (ρ≈−0.9) — per-source attribution simply does not exist in this literature; (iv) doing all this in a PRODIGY-style in-context regime, where the pretraining-data question has never been asked.

**Best contrast claims:** (1) "SAMGPT reports monotone gains from adding source domains (Table 4) and AnyGraph claims scaling-law emergence — we show apparent corpus gains are coverage effects: performance ≈ MAX over components, and curves flatten once the best donor for a target is in the mix." (2) "GCOPE/GFT/MDGPT treat negative transfer as a pathology to engineer away — we show composition is lawfully max-not-sum, so the marginal value of a new source is predictable (≈0 unless it is a better donor)." (3) "LLM data-mixing laws fit smooth functions of mixture proportions (2403.16952, 2507.09404) — graph corpus composition is instead discrete/max-like, closer to the curse of multilinguality (2311.09205)."

**Must-cite to avoid looking ignorant:** PRODIGY 2305.12600, OFA 2310.00149, GCOPE 2402.09834, position paper 2402.02216, APT 2311.01038, 2402.02054 + 2408.11243, SAMGPT 2502.05424, PromptGFM 2503.03313, One-Model-One-Graph 2412.00315, GraphFM 2407.11907, AnyGraph 2408.10700, GFT 2411.06070, Data Mixing Laws 2403.16952 + DoReMi 2305.10429 + RegMix 2407.01492, XLM-R 1911.02116 (+2010.03017), ULTRA 2310.04562, and at least one GFM survey (2505.15116 or 2503.09363).
