# Paper program schema

Use this schema only when no existing manuscript claim ledger or submission plan can
serve as the canonical program view. Adapt an existing artifact in place when possible.

## Portfolio

Record each plausible manuscript with:

- stable name and path;
- status: active, candidate, paused, superseded, submitted, or accepted;
- target venue and deadline when actually chosen;
- one-sentence thesis;
- reason for its current status;
- relationship to overlapping manuscripts.

Exactly one manuscript should normally be active. If none or several are active, make
that a visible decision rather than resolving it implicitly.

## Active manuscript

Record:

- thesis;
- contribution boundary: what the paper does and does not claim;
- intended audience or venue, if decided;
- central claims in narrative order;
- paper-level completion criteria;
- current binding blocker;
- primary next action;
- last evidence review date and reviewed revisions of both repositories.

## Claim record

For every central claim, record:

- claim identifier and manuscript wording;
- importance: essential or supporting;
- status: supported, provisional, contradicted, missing, or invalidated;
- evidence threshold decided before new results when possible;
- supporting and conflicting evidence with repository paths and revisions;
- strongest alternative explanation or reviewer objection;
- missing decision, if any;
- next action and stopping condition;
- destination section, figure, or table;
- integration status.

## Work item record

For every admitted experiment or synthesis task, record:

- linked manuscript and claim;
- uncertainty or reviewer risk addressed;
- planned comparison and controlled variables;
- outcomes that would strengthen, weaken, or not affect the claim;
- decision consequence for each material outcome;
- cheapest valid scope and stopping condition;
- lifecycle state;
- authoritative setup, analysis, and evidence paths;
- producing revision and artifact provenance;
- manuscript integration target.

## Parking lot

Keep nonessential ideas separate from admitted work. Record only the idea, potential
paper relevance, and condition that would justify promotion. Do not give parked ideas
the operational detail or apparent priority of active work.

## Review cadence

At a program review:

1. Revalidate the active manuscript choice.
2. Reassess central claims against committed evidence, including negative evidence.
3. Close or downgrade claims that cannot be defended.
4. Integrate completed evidence before admitting more work.
5. Stop campaigns that no longer change a paper decision.
6. Select one primary next action and a bounded secondary action.

The program artifact should become shorter and more decisive as the paper converges.
